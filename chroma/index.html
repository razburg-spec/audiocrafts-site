Chroma

Six compressors. One knob.

Every position of the Character knob is a complete compressor, not a preset: its own detector, knee, release law and saturation circuit. Vintage listens slowly and lets go like a light cell. Indie hits hard and, past 10:1, folds over into pumping. Bus holds steady through a long squeeze and snaps back after a stray peak. It opens with no processing.

Features

Everything a compressor needs. Nothing it doesn't.

The controls are classic and stay put. The character lives underneath them.

Releases with a memory

Vintage lets go more slowly the longer and harder it has been working, then takes seconds to forget. Bus runs a slow and a fast envelope together, so a sustained squeeze holds while single peaks bounce straight back.

Detectors that listen differently

Vintage and Chubby average the signal and react to loudness; Retro, Indie and Modern catch every peak. Knees run from Indie's hard corner to Chubby's 20 dB curve that starts working before you hear it.

Saturation with an accent

Each character has its own shaper and its own band: even-order tube warmth kept under 9 kHz, thick low harmonics, a sharp-kneed grit from 150 Hz up, or distortion that rises with the gain reduction. Oversampled, so it never turns harsh.

Parallel, done right

The blend happens before the saturation, so the dry and compressed paths pass through the same filters and stay perfectly aligned, and the colour stays the same at any mix.

Soft Clip

Does nothing below about -4.4 dBFS. Above it, peaks are rounded into a -0.2 dB ceiling instead of being cut.

Key, two ways

Your host's sidechain for ducking and pumping against the kick, or Freq Key: a narrow band from 50 Hz to 16 kHz drives the detector, to tame sibilance or one boomy note. Listen lets you hear exactly what it hears.

Characters

Six voices on one knob.

Each one blends the traits of a few classic designs into a single voice. Turn the knob while the track plays.

Vintage

A light-cell leveler with a tube make-up stage. Wide knee, an averaging detector, an attack that never gets faster than 6 ms so transients slip through, and a release that slows the longer it works. Warm, even-order colour.

Retro

The attitude of a fast transistor limiter: near-instant attack, a tight knee and a bright, forward edge. Its distortion grows as the gain reduction deepens, so the harder it works, the more it bites.

Chubby

Variable-tube weight. A knee so wide it is squeezing before you notice, a slow detector and thick, low-heavy harmonics. Gentle at first, firmer the harder you push.

Indie

Hard knee, peak detection, a quick release and a sharp, gritty clipper. Past 10:1 the curve bends down, so louder in comes out quieter: the pump of a crushed room mic.

Modern

Clean, peak-accurate control with a moderate knee and full-range response. Saturation adds only a soft, open sheen.

Bus

Console glue for the whole mix. A gentle knee, a short averaging window and a dual release that rides the music rather than the peaks, with a faint full-range console colour.
